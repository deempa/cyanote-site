Reminders not syncing between iPhone and Mac: start with what's missing

By Cyanote · 23 November 2026 · 11 min read
  • Tasks
  • macOS
  • How-to

When Reminders aren't syncing between an iPhone and a Mac, first find out what's missing. A whole list usually means a different account, or Reminders switched off under iCloud on one device. A single item is usually hidden as completed, filtered out, or in another list. If the item is there but never alerted you, sync is working and the fault is in the Mac's notification settings. Leave signing out of iCloud until last.

This is a reading of Apple's guides for macOS 27 and iOS 27, checked on 23 November 2026. The Mac paths I compared with the macOS 26 guide match. Where a claim comes from a forum post instead, I say so.

The short version

  • Same Apple Account, Reminders on, on both devices. Mac: System Settings → your name → iCloud → See All → Reminders.
  • Look at the account headings. A list in a work Exchange account follows that account, not iCloud, and some Microsoft work accounts have stopped syncing on the Mac since October.
  • Check the plain list, not a Smart List. Completed items are hidden, and custom Smart Lists only show what matches their filters.
  • An alert on the phone but not on the Mac is a notification problem. An Urgent alarm is meant to sound on the iPhone or iPad.
  • Clear overdue items on every device. Apple lists this as a sync fix. Clearing completed ones deletes your history, so leave that until later.
  • Print your lists before you toggle anything off. A copy kept after signing out stops updating.

One test splits the problem in half. Open icloud.com/reminders (Apple's guide) in a browser. If the reminder is there but not on the Mac, the Mac isn't receiving. If it's on the phone but not on the website, the phone isn't sending.

There are two exceptions. Apple says you might not see all your shared lists on the website, so don't judge a shared list by it. And if you use Advanced Data Protection, web access is turned off unless you turned it back on.

The whole list is missing

Work down this list. Nothing here deletes anything.

  1. Check Apple first. System Status has a separate line for iCloud Reminders. If it isn't green, wait.
  2. Compare the accounts. Apple's sync article wants the same Apple Account on every device, current software, and a correct date and time.
  3. Check the switch on both. On the Mac it's System Settings → your name → iCloud → See All → Reminders, and Apple's setup page gives that path for macOS 15 and later. On the iPhone it's Settings → your name → iCloud → See All → Reminders.
  4. Read the account headings. With one account, the Mac sidebar says My Lists. With several, each account is named, and a list lives in only one of them. Add that account on the other device in System Settings → Internet Accounts, or on the iPhone in Settings → Apps → Reminders → Reminders Accounts.

If the missing list belongs to a work Microsoft account and vanished from the Mac this autumn, stop toggling iCloud. Apple says Reminders accounts on macOS may no longer sync with Exchange from October 2026, and that the person to ask is your Exchange administrator. I explain why the iPhone carries on working in calendar not syncing between iPhone and Mac.

You can't fix a wrong-account list by dragging it: Apple says you can't drag a list to a different account. Make a new list under iCloud instead, select the reminders, then copy and paste them across. Check that the copies reached the other device before you delete the originals.

An old device that was never going to see it

iCloud Reminders changed format with iOS 13 and macOS Catalina. Once an account is updated, its reminders aren't compatible with earlier systems, and an older Mac can only see them on iCloud.com. Shared lists need the creator and everyone viewing them to have updated. If Reminders shows an Update button, it hasn't been updated yet, but don't press it until you've checked what your oldest device runs. Apple also warns that on a device that's just been upgraded, reminders you make (with Siri, say) before opening Reminders for the first time are lost when you open it.

A smaller version of this affects custom Smart Lists. Those that use certain filters, such as Any tag or No Date, are hidden on macOS 12 and iOS 15 or earlier. The reminders themselves stay in their original lists.

The list is there but the item isn't

Most of these aren't sync faults.

  • It was completed. A finished reminder is saved but hidden. On the Mac, scroll to the top of the list and click Show.
  • You're in a Smart List. Today and Scheduled show only reminders with a date. Select All, which covers every account.
  • It went to the default list. Reminders made with Siri, from another app, or inside a Smart List land in the default list. If the phone's default sits in a work account, Siri's reminders go to work. Set it to an iCloud list in Settings → Apps → Reminders → Default List on the iPhone and Reminders → Settings on the Mac.
  • It was deleted somewhere. Check Recently Deleted. The Mac guide says items stay there for 30 days.

If the item really is in an iCloud list and still isn't on the Mac, quit Reminders and reopen it. On the iPhone, Apple says to close the app, wait about a minute, and open it again. One MacRumors forum post from September 2024 describes a Mac that only caught up after a reopen, and the step costs nothing. Restart both devices next.

The reminder is there but the Mac never alerted you

The iPhone buzzes, the Mac stays silent, and it looks like a sync failure. Open Reminders on the Mac. If the reminder is there with the right time, sync did its job and one of these gates stopped the alert.

  • Notifications. In System Settings → Notifications → Reminders, turn on Allow notifications. Apple's settings page for macOS 27 and 26 has separate options for Desktop, Notification Center and Lock Screen, and a Temporary or Persistent style. The Reminders guide still uses the old names, Banners and Alerts.
  • Screen sharing. The same page has a switch that pauses notifications while you mirror or share the display.
  • Focus. A Focus can silence Reminders. Add it under System Settings → Focus → your Focus → Allowed Apps. To let time-sensitive alerts through, both the Focus and the app have to allow them.
  • All-day reminders. With a date and no time, an alert needs Today Notification set in Reminders → Settings.
  • Urgent. Marking a reminder Urgent sets off an alarm on either your iPhone or iPad, whichever is set as your location in Find My. There's no Mac alarm to fix, and Apple doesn't say whether the Mac shows an ordinary notification as well.
  • Place-based reminders. These need Location Services on each device.

A shared list isn't updating

Both people must accept the invitation, use iCloud, and have updated reminders. If the list disappeared altogether, someone may have stopped sharing it: Apple says that removes it from every participant's devices.

Missed alerts are often by design. Apple states that "notifications aren't shared", so a time you set reminds you and nobody else. What you can turn on is a notice when items are added or completed, in the list's Participants window under Notify When.

Old reminders keep coming back

Apple's sync article tells you to open Today, complete or delete anything overdue, and repeat that on each device. On the Mac the step is headed "Delete completed or overdue reminders". It doesn't explain why that helps.

Clearing finished items is the next step, and it deletes your record of what you've done. Print it first if you use it. Then, on the Mac, select the Completed Smart List, scroll to the top and click Clear.

I found one first-hand account, and it isn't encouraging. A MacRumors poster in February 2026, on beta software, cut a backlog of more than 6,000 completed items because active ones kept flipping to completed, and was still asking for a fix. Apple publishes a limit of 50,000 items for calendars and reminders together, but says it doesn't apply once reminders have been updated. So there's no documented number of completed reminders that breaks sync.

Before you switch iCloud Reminders off or sign out

Apple's sync article doesn't suggest either of these for an iPhone or a Mac. Leave them until last, because anything that exists only on one device may never have been uploaded.

Switching Reminders off. For a single account, Apple says the reminders stop appearing but stay in the account, naming iCloud, and on the Mac they reappear when you turn it back on. Neither page mentions items that were never uploaded, so assume those are at risk and read any prompt you get.

Signing out. Signing out of your Apple Account on a Mac asks whether to keep copies of Reminders data. Keep them and they're no longer updated from iCloud. Decline and they're removed from the Mac. Either way, what's in iCloud stays there and comes back when you sign in.

Do this first:

  1. Check at iCloud.com that your important lists are there.
  2. On the Mac, open each list and use File → Print, then save it as a PDF. You can't restore from it, but you'll have the words.
  3. Switch Reminders off only on the device that's wrong. Restart it, then switch Reminders back on.

iCloud also keeps archived versions you can restore on iCloud.com. That rolls your calendars back too, and removes sharing from every shared list and calendar. Apple says the current version is archived first, so you can go back to it. Try Recently Deleted before this: another Apple page says a reminder deleted after the format update can't be recovered at iCloud.com.

How to tell it's fixed

Make a reminder called "from phone" in an iCloud list, and one called "from Mac" in the same list. Wait a few minutes with both apps open. Tick each one off on the other device. If both ticks cross over, new items, completions and both directions all work.

Questions people ask

Why do my reminders show in Calendar but not in Reminders?

Calendar only shows scheduled reminders, from the same Reminders data. If you can see one there, look in Scheduled or All in Reminders. Turning off Scheduled Reminders in Calendar's list only hides them in Calendar.

Does iCloud storage affect Reminders?

Apple's iCloud settings page says that when storage is almost full some apps may fail to sync, and when it's full new items don't save to iCloud. It doesn't single out Reminders, but it's worth a look.

My notes don't sync either. Is it the same fault?

It could be, if the account or storage is at fault. Notes has its own switch and its own local account, so check it separately with Apple Notes not syncing.
