Leaving Bear, and what the subscription actually buys

By Lior Rabanian · 1 September 2026 · 4 min read
  • Notes
  • Buying advice
  • One-time purchase

Bear is the best-looking notes app on the Mac and it is not a close contest either. The typography is considered, the Markdown is unobtrusive, the tag system is genuinely clever, and it feels like a native app because it is one.

People do not leave Bear because it is bad. They leave because of one recurring line on a bank statement, and it is worth being exact about what that line is paying for before deciding it is not worth it.

What is free and what is not

From bear.app, checked 1 September 2026: Bear Pro is $2.99 a month or $29.99 a year, with a seven-day trial.

The free tier is more useful than most people assume. You get local access to all your notes, document scanning, export to a long list of formats — TXT, Markdown, TextBundle, RTF, PDF, JPG, HTML, DOCX, ePub — three themes and one app icon.

Pro adds 28-plus themes and fifteen app icons, OCR search inside PDFs and images, and the one that actually matters: iCloud sync across your devices.

So the honest summary is that Bear's subscription is a sync subscription with cosmetics attached. If you use Bear on one Mac and nowhere else, the free tier is a complete notes app and you should not be paying at all.

Which means the question is about sync

$29.99 a year for sync across a Mac, an iPad and an iPhone is not a rip-off. Someone maintains the sync engine, handles conflicts, and keeps it working across three OS updates a year. That is real work with a real ongoing cost, and the arithmetic of a one-time price explains why an app that syncs cannot honestly charge once.

The question is only whether you use it.

A large number of Bear subscriptions are paid by people who capture on their phone two or three times a month and do everything else on one Mac. That is $29.99 a year for a convenience you could replace with the share sheet and an email to yourself — and there are several other routes to getting a note onto your phone that cost nothing.

If, on the other hand, you write on an iPad and edit on a Mac and check things on a phone, sync is not a convenience, it is the product. Keep paying. Nothing local will replace it.

The tag system is the switching cost

This is the part people underestimate.

Bear does not have folders. It has nested tags written inline in the note — #work/clients/acme — and over a couple of years those become the structure of your entire collection. They are not metadata sitting beside the note. They are text inside it.

That is elegant while you are in Bear and awkward when you leave. Export to Markdown and your tags come with you as literal #work/clients/acme strings in the body, which is honest but not useful: the receiving app will show them as text, or as its own flat tags, or as headings if you are unlucky.

Before you move anything, look at how much of your organisation lives in tags rather than in the notes themselves. If the answer is "most of it", you are not migrating notes, you are migrating a taxonomy, and it will need rebuilding by hand. What actually travels when you leave an app is worth reading first.

Do the export before you decide

Bear's export is good, which is a genuine mark in its favour and also the reason there is no excuse for skipping this.

Export the whole library to Markdown, in batches. Not to PDF, not to Bear's own archive format if you intend to read it elsewhere.

Open five of the files at random in something that is not Bear. This is where you find out how images were referenced and what happened to your tables.

Keep the export whether you stay or not. It costs a few megabytes and it turns a subscription into something you can walk away from on your own schedule rather than at renewal.

Who should not switch

If you use Bear on more than one device, stay. This is the whole argument and it is decisive.

If the free tier already covers you and you are not paying, there is nothing to save and no reason to move. Bear free on one Mac is an excellent situation.

If the typography is why you use it — and for some people it genuinely is — nothing else on the Mac looks like Bear, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

The honest version

Cyanote is a worse-looking notes app than Bear. It has no iOS app, no iCloud sync, no themes worth boasting about, and if writing in a beautiful window is the point, this is a downgrade.

What it does instead is put notes, to-dos, a board, a calendar, habits, routines and a system-wide clipboard manager in one window on one Mac, in one SQLite file you own, with no account, for $4.99 once. Against Bear Pro at $29.99 a year, that pays for itself in about two months — but only if you were paying for sync you were not using.

If you were using the sync, the comparison is not about money and this is the wrong app for you.
