How to recover deleted notes on Mac, after checking they were deleted at all

By Cyanote · 23 November 2026 · 11 min read
  • Notes
  • macOS
  • How-to
  • Backups

To recover deleted notes on a Mac, open Notes, click Recently Deleted in the sidebar, and drag the note into another folder. Apple keeps it there for up to 30 days. If that folder is missing or the note isn't in it, search All Accounts and check the note's account is still switched on, because many missing notes were never deleted. After 30 days, the realistic route is a backup you already had.

I make Cyanote, a notes app, which gets a short section near the end. I don't sell recovery software. This is a reading of Apple's guides for macOS 27 and macOS 26, checked on 23 November 2026, not a lab test. The retention terms are the same in Apple's macOS 27 and macOS 26 guides. One menu name changed: View → Show Sidebar is View → Show Folders on macOS 26.

The short version

  • Do nothing destructive first. Don't empty Recently Deleted, remove an account or clear a note's text while you're looking.
  • Deleted or hidden? An account may be off, or the search limited to one account.
  • Within 30 days: Recently Deleted, on the Mac, on iCloud.com or on any device using the same account.
  • Gmail, Yahoo and other mail accounts: Notes has no Recently Deleted folder for these. Look in that account's own Trash, and in Gmail's All Mail.
  • Overwritten rather than deleted: Recently Deleted won't have it. Undo, or a backup, are the options.
  • Recovery software: on a modern Mac's SSD, for a note inside a synced database, I wouldn't pay for it.

Three of those deserve more detail.

A switched-off account. Apple's accounts page says the account's notes "aren't available while Notes is turned off", and that turning Notes back on shows them again. Removing the account deletes its notes from that Mac, but copies remain in the internet account. My explainer on iCloud Notes covers how several accounts sit in one sidebar.

Search. Open the search field, click the control beside the magnifying glass, and choose All Accounts. Apple's search page says a locked note shows only its title in results, even while your locked notes are unlocked. Notes in Recently Deleted are included, so check the folder shown with each hit.

A locked note you can't open isn't deleted, but it may still be lost. If you forget a custom password and can't use Touch ID, Apple's lock page says it can't help you regain access.

Recover it from Recently Deleted on the Mac

  1. Open Notes. If you can't see the sidebar, choose View → Show Sidebar.
  2. Click Recently Deleted, which is always last in its account. If you use both iCloud and On My Mac, check each one.
  3. Drag the note to another folder in the same account. Apple warns that moving notes between accounts can permanently lose formatting and attachments.

If the note is in its new folder and you can type in it, you have it back.

Pages online disagree about 30 or 40 days, and both numbers are Apple's. For upgraded iCloud notes, the guide says that after 30 days the notes "are removed from all your devices" and after 40 days they "are permanently deleted from iCloud". Apple's iCloud guide says only that notes left there are permanently removed after 30. I found no documented way for you to reach a note between day 30 and day 40, so plan on 30.

Never clear a note's text in that folder. Apple says deleting or replacing a note's contents effectively deletes it right away.

Check iCloud.com as well

iCloud.com shows the same account without relying on your Mac having synced.

  1. Go to icloud.com/notes and sign in.
  2. Select Recently Deleted in the folder list.
  3. Select the note, then Recover in the toolbar. It moves to the Notes folder.

If Recently Deleted isn't listed, Apple's iCloud guide says there's nothing to recover there. Notes you permanently removed can't come back this way. If the site says web access is off, as it is by default with Advanced Data Protection, turn it on in iCloud settings and approve it from a trusted device.

Shared notes follow different rules

The result depends on who owned the note, according to Apple's page on deleting shared notes:

  • You own it and deleted it. It disappears from every participant's devices, and you can recover it from your Recently Deleted folder.
  • Someone shared it with you and you deleted it. It leaves your list only, and never reaches your Recently Deleted folder. Open the original link again, which works unless the owner stopped sharing.
  • The owner deleted it. It's in the owner's Recently Deleted, not yours, so ask within 30 days.

Notes kept in Gmail, Yahoo or another mail account

The Mac guide says that outside upgraded iCloud notes, deleted notes are "removed permanently from all devices that use the same account", with no Recently Deleted folder. Apple's missing notes article, written for iPhone, differs: it says a third-party account "will usually keep it in the Trash folder".

So look in that account's Trash in Mail, or on the provider's website. Apple says you have to copy the text and paste it into a new note. Gmail keeps deleted items in Trash for up to 30 days.

If it isn't in Gmail's Trash, search All Mail. For anything a mail client deletes over IMAP, Gmail has a setting: archive, move to Trash, or delete for good. Archived items are in All Mail. Neither company says which of these a deleted Apple note follows, so check both.

Restoring from Time Machine: read this first

A mistake here can overwrite the notes you have now, and syncing can undo the rescue. Use it only for something you can't replace, and set up these three things before you start:

  • Go offline. Turn off Wi-Fi and unplug Ethernet, and stay offline until your current notes are back in place. Martin Fowler wrote in 2014 that iCloud overwrote his restored version with the current one. Nothing Apple publishes says that has changed.
  • Use a disk plugged into this Mac. If your backup is on the network, going offline cuts it off, so don't use the steps below.
  • Copy your current notes folder first (step 2), with enough free space for it.

Apple documents that Time Machine keeps hourly copies for a day, daily for a month, then weekly, and that restored items return to their original location. I found no Apple page on restoring the Notes database. The steps below follow Macworld's, from May 2024, which warns "it will be trivial to overwrite all your current notes". Going offline, and steps 5 and 6's extra care, are my additions.

  1. Go offline and quit Notes.
  2. In the Finder, use Go → Go to Folder to open ~/Library/Group Containers/. Option-drag group.com.apple.notes to the Desktop. Wait for it to finish, and use File → Get Info on both to compare their sizes. Don't continue without this copy.
  3. Choose Browse Time Machine Backups from the Time Machine menu, go back to before the deletion, select the same folder and click Restore.
  4. Open Notes and copy the text you came for into a document saved outside Notes. Change nothing else.
  5. Quit Notes. Move the restored folder out to a new folder on the Desktop, then Option-drag your copy from step 2 back, so the Desktop keeps one too.
  6. Open Notes, still offline, and check that your newest notes are there. Then reconnect. Keep both Desktop copies until you're sure.

If you're comfortable in Terminal, there's a route that never touches the live folder. The tmutil restore command, according to its manual page on macOS 27.2, copies an item out of a backup to a place you choose, and may need Full Disk Access. The open-source Apple Cloud Notes Parser can then rebuild the notes in that copied folder as HTML. Its README says it opens a copy of the database and needs the password for locked notes, and that it can't read ones locked with a device passcode. It lists versions up to 26, so treat a macOS 27 database as unproven.

Is data recovery software worth it?

For a deleted Apple note on a recent Mac, I think not, for two reasons.

First, a note isn't a file in a folder. The parser's README describes Notes data as "a series of protobufs and tables in the database". Deleting one note changes a database that's still there, so software that looks for deleted files is looking for the wrong thing.

Second, SSDs erase deleted data. Howard Oakley at Eclectic Light explains that macOS tells the drive which blocks are free, and that start-up is one time this is known to happen. He calls the chances on Apple silicon and T2 Macs "diminishingly small" unless a snapshot holds the files. Time Machine's local snapshots last up to 24 hours, so speed matters more than software.

The exception is an older Mac with a spinning hard disk, where Oakley says results can be surprisingly good.

Stickies and Quick Notes

Stickies notes are separate from Notes. Apple's guide says they only appear while Stickies is open, so open it before worrying. It mentions no bin for deleted ones. File → Export All to Notes copies what's left into Notes, as covered in my Stickies guide.

Quick Notes live in Notes, in a Quick Notes folder.

So the next one is easier

Sync copies a deletion everywhere, so it isn't a backup. Two habits cover most of the risk: Time Machine to an external disk, and an occasional export of your notes kept somewhere else. My guide to backing up notes on your own computer has the rest.

Questions people ask

Can I recover permanently deleted notes on a Mac?

Only from a copy that already exists elsewhere: a Time Machine backup, an export, an email you sent yourself, or another person's copy. Apple's iCloud guide says you can't recover notes you permanently removed.

Why is there no Recently Deleted folder on my Mac?

It only appears if a note was deleted in the last 30 days. Accounts other than upgraded iCloud or On My Mac don't have one. An Upgrade button beside iCloud in the sidebar means that account hasn't been upgraded.

Can I get back an earlier version of a note I changed?

Not inside Notes. Shared notes have an activity list, but Glenn Fleishman at Six Colors found deletions aren't marked and saw no way to roll back.
