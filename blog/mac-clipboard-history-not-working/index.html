Mac clipboard history not working: find the symptom, then the fix

By Cyanote · 23 November 2026 · 11 min read
  • Clipboard
  • macOS
  • How-to

If your Mac clipboard history isn't working, check three things first. You need macOS 26 Tahoe or later. Results from Clipboard must be on in System Settings → Spotlight. And the time limit there may have hidden your items: the choices are 30 minutes, 8 hours or 7 days. If all that's right, copy a word in TextEdit and press ⌘ + Space, then ⌘ + 4. If it's missing, install updates and restart.

I make Cyanote, which has a clipboard history of its own, so I'm not neutral about the built-in one. Most of this post is about getting Apple's version working, because it's free and already on your Mac. It's a reading of Apple's documentation and of user reports, checked on 23 November 2026, and I mark which is which.

The short version

  • No Clipboard button: check the macOS version, then turn on Results from Clipboard.
  • Items vanish: raise the limit under Clipboard history is available in Spotlight. Seven days is the most Apple offers.
  • Only some copies are missing: password-manager copies, long text and copies from Mail are the ones reported as skipped.
  • The shortcut opens something else: Raycast, Alfred or an input source has taken ⌘ + Space.
  • The list is empty even for a fresh copy: install updates, restart, then work through the Spotlight steps below.

Step 1: check the version and turn it on

The history arrived with macOS 26 Tahoe. Choose Apple menu → About This Mac to see what you have. On macOS 15 or earlier there's nothing to repair, because the feature isn't there.

On macOS 26 or 27, open System Settings → Spotlight and scroll to the bottom. Apple's Spotlight settings page describes Results from Clipboard as the switch that allows "the contents of your Clipboard to appear in Spotlight search results." Two naming differences can throw you:

  • On early Tahoe, the switch was called Clipboard Search, according to the macOS 26 version of that page.
  • The sidebar item is Spotlight on some versions and Siri & Spotlight on others. Apple's Spotlight indexing article gives both.

It may also have asked you once, and you may have said no. Apple's clipboard history guide says that the first time you search the clipboard you may be asked to allow it, and to click Allow. The Tahoe version says Enable in Spotlight instead.

Turning it on can't bring back earlier copies. Cult of Mac notes that it stores your history "from that point onward", so copy something new before you decide it's still broken.

Step 2: take the shortcut back

There's no separate shortcut for clipboard history out of the box. You open Spotlight with ⌘ + Space and then press ⌘ + 4, both listed in Apple's Spotlight shortcuts. If something else answers ⌘ + Space, you never reach it.

Launchers are the usual reason. Raycast's manual recommends giving ⌘ + Space to Raycast and unticking Show Spotlight search. Alfred's help has similar steps, and adds that macOS can reserve the combination for switching input languages.

To check, open System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts and look under Spotlight and Input Sources. Apple's page on conflicting shortcuts says a warning icon appears beside any that clash. Either free the key in the launcher, or give Spotlight a different one.

Don't want to change anything? Click the Spotlight icon in the menu bar and then the Clipboard button. If that works, the history was fine all along.

Step 3: look at the time limit

Older guides disagree about this, so here's what Apple's current settings page says. Under Clipboard history is available in Spotlight, you "choose 30 minutes, 8 hours, or 7 days", and anything older "won't be visible in Spotlight." If you've seen 30 days quoted, Apple's list doesn't include it.

Apple doesn't name the default. Cult of Mac and Make Tech Easier both report it as 8 hours. The choice itself only arrived in macOS 26.1. Before that, Spotlight kept items for up to eight hours and you couldn't change it.

So something you copied yesterday morning will be gone today on the reported default. Set it to 7 days, copy a word, and look again tomorrow. That's also the trade: a week of copies stays searchable by anyone using your unlocked Mac. Look through what a clipboard history shouldn't remember before you raise it.

There's one more way for items to vanish. Clear History, under the button beside Spotlight's search field, and Clear Clipboard History in settings both empty the list. Neither Apple page mentions an undo.

Passwords missing is the feature working properly. The developer's test is worth knowing about if you wonder why one password manager's copies appear and another's don't: plain text showed up, and the same text labelled as concealed didn't.

I found no setting that adds any of these back. For Mail, or a long passage, paste it into a note as you go. Universal Clipboard is separate too. Apple says a copy from another device stays briefly, or until you replace it, so paste it on the Mac straight away.

In July 2026 one MacRumors forum user said that "most of the items are missing" and couldn't find a pattern. If that's you, go on to step 5.

Step 5: when the list is empty

If a plain word you just copied in TextEdit doesn't appear, none of the causes above explains it. Give the list a few seconds first: one MacRumors poster in June 2026 found that items took several seconds to load on macOS 26 and, later, almost none on a macOS 27 beta. Then try these in order.

  1. Install updates. Use System Settings → General → Software Update, per Apple. iDownloadBlog's writer had a clipboard history that didn't work for several weeks on early macOS 26, until an update fixed it.
  2. Turn it off and on. Apple doesn't say what happens to saved items, so paste anything you need into a note first. Then switch Results from Clipboard off and on.
  3. Turn on Documents. If you've trimmed Spotlight's categories, look for Documents in the same pane and switch it on. A user in Apple's now-closed forum said that fixed it. I've only seen that post as a search excerpt, and the step loses nothing.
  4. Save your work, restart, and log in with your password. Another post there, also seen only as an excerpt, said the history stopped after sleep and a Touch ID unlock, and came back after a restart and a typed password. Treat it as one person's finding.
  5. Try Safe Mode. If the history works there, something that starts with your Mac is interfering. Apple's Safe Mode guide has the steps for both kinds of Mac.
  6. Clear it and start again. This empties the list for good, so save what you need from it. Then click Clear Clipboard History.
  7. Rebuild Spotlight's index, last. Apple's instructions are to add your disk under Search Privacy, wait a few seconds, and remove it. Don't leave the disk on the list: Apple warns that excluding your whole internal disk stops update notifications. Apple says indexing can take "hours or even days", and describes the fix for files, not for the clipboard.

You'll also see killall pboard suggested. It restarts the service behind copy and paste, and I found no evidence that it repairs the history. Use it when copying itself has failed.

Step 6: when an item won't paste

Apple's guide describes a two-part move: click the copy button beside an item, or select it and press ⌘ + C, then click where you want it and press ⌘ + V. Cult of Mac says Return pastes straight into the app you were using. If Return does nothing, use Apple's version and then test the paste in TextEdit.

If it pastes in TextEdit but not where you wanted it, the history did its job and that field refused it. If it pastes without its formatting, that's how the history behaves. Copy from the original again when you need the styling.

On a work Mac

I searched Apple's published device management schemas and found no setting that controls Spotlight's clipboard history. That isn't proof your employer can't limit it some other way. If the switch is missing or keeps turning itself off, ask IT before trying anything in step 5.

If it's a clipboard manager that stopped recording

Separate apps fail for different reasons, and usually document them.

  • It records but won't paste. Look for its Accessibility permission. Maccy's README says to check Paste automatically and then System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. I've explained why these apps need it.
  • It stopped after an update. Alfred's troubleshooting page says macOS can "forget" the permission, and that removing the app from the list and adding it again resolves it.
  • It ignores one app. Check the ignore list. Alfred skips Keychain Access and 1Password on purpose, and Maccy can be paused altogether.
  • The whole history is odd. Alfred's last resort is clearing its history, which rebuilds the database. You lose the saved copies, though not your snippets.

Questions people ask

Does Mac clipboard history survive a restart?

Apple's pages don't say. They describe the time limit and the clear button and nothing about restarting. Don't rely on it for anything you'd hate to lose.

Can I make Spotlight keep clipboard items for longer than 7 days?

Not with the settings Apple documents. Anything you'll want next month belongs in a note or a pinned snippet.

How do I see clipboard history on an older Mac?

You need a separate app on macOS 15 and earlier. Finder's Edit → Show Clipboard shows only the current item. My guide to clipboard history on a Mac covers what each version has.
