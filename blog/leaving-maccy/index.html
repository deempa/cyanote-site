Leaving Maccy, when free stops being the point

By Lior Rabanian · 1 September 2026 · 5 min read
  • Clipboard
  • Buying advice
  • macOS

Maccy is the app I recommend to people who ask me for a clipboard manager and do not want to spend anything. That is not a diplomatic sentence. It is free, it is MIT-licensed and the whole source is on GitHub, it opens instantly, it stays out of the way, and it has been maintained for years by someone who clearly uses it.

So a post about leaving it has to start by being honest: most people reading this should stay. What follows is the small set of reasons that are actually structural, as opposed to the feature envy that makes people churn through tools without ever being better off.

What Maccy gets right, and why it is hard to beat

It does one job with almost no surface. Hit the hotkey, the history appears, type to filter, press return. Pinning is there — ⌥P moves an item to the top with a permanent shortcut. It polls the pasteboard every 500 ms by default, which is invisible in practice and cheap enough that you forget it is running.

That minimalism is the product. Every paid clipboard manager on the Mac has to justify itself against an app that costs nothing and does the core thing well, and most of them justify it with features you will use twice.

Be suspicious of anything that tries to sell you a replacement on the basis that Maccy is missing something. Usually it is not missing it. You just did not need it.

The reasons that are actually structural

It requires a recent macOS. Current Maccy needs macOS Sonoma 14 or later. If you are on an older Mac that Apple stopped feeding updates to, this is not a preference, it is a wall — and it is the one reason on this list that has nothing to do with taste. What still runs well on an older Mac is a longer version of that problem.

It has no sync, and neither does anything honest at this price. If you copy on a desktop and paste on a laptop, no free local clipboard manager solves that. Paste does, by running a sync service and charging for it. That is a real difference and it is worth paying for if it is your situation.

The clipboard is one of five things you are running. This is the reason that actually moves people, and it is not a criticism of Maccy at all. Maccy holds the clips, something else holds the notes, something else the tasks, something else the calendar. Each one is fine. The cost is the crossing between them, which nobody counts because it is two seconds at a time.

The crossing is the thing worth measuring

Watch what you actually do with a clip you want to keep.

You copy something — a command, an address, a paragraph from a doc you will lose access to. It goes into the clipboard history. Then, if it matters, you open your notes app, find or make the right note, and paste it there. The clipboard was a corridor, not a destination.

A clipboard manager cannot help with the second half of that, because it does not know what a note is. It is doing its job perfectly and the friction is in the seam. This is the same argument as when a free clipboard manager stops being enough, stated from the other end: the ceiling is not the history length, it is that the history is a different app from the place things get kept.

If you never do that second half — if your clipboard is genuinely a scratch buffer and nothing in it is worth keeping — then Maccy is not just adequate, it is correct, and a bundled alternative buys you nothing.

What to check before you move anything

Your history does not migrate. No clipboard manager imports another's history, and you should not want it to — a clipboard history is the most disposable data you own, and some of what is in it should not be kept at all. Whatever you switch to starts empty. Treat that as a feature.

Your pins do. Manually, and it takes five minutes. Open Maccy, look at what you pinned, and write those down. That list is the only thing you genuinely lose, and it is usually shorter than you expect — the snippets you actually paste every week are rarely more than a dozen.

Two clipboard managers running at once is a bad afternoon. They both poll the pasteboard, they both pop up on hotkeys that will collide, and the second one will capture what the first one just captured. Pick one, quit the other properly, and remove it from login items before you judge the replacement.

Who should not switch

If you want a clipboard manager and nothing else, stay. Free beats $4.99, and paying for a bundle whose other four parts you will not open is a worse deal than paying nothing.

If you need the history on two machines, neither Maccy nor Cyanote helps you, and this is the case where Paste's subscription is honestly earning its money. The longer comparison is here.

If you are on macOS 13 or earlier and Maccy will not run, the answer is an older build or a different app — not necessarily a paid one.

The honest version

Cyanote's argument is not that it is a better clipboard manager than Maccy. On the narrow job, they are close enough that the difference is taste, and Maccy costs nothing.

The argument is that the clipboard sits in the same window as your notes, to-dos, calendar and habits, in one SQLite file on your own Mac, with no account, for $4.99 once. A clip becomes a note without crossing an app boundary, and the four things you were running separately become one thing you were going to open anyway.

If that describes the friction you actually feel, it is worth the price. If it does not, Maccy is free, it is good, and there is no reason to change.
