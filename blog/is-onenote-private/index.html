Is OneNote private? Who can read your notebooks

By Cyanote · 23 November 2026 · 12 min read
  • Privacy
  • Notes
  • Windows

OneNote is private from other people, but not from Microsoft, and on a work account not from your employer. Notebooks in OneDrive are encrypted with keys Microsoft holds, not end-to-end. The exception is a password-protected section, which Microsoft says its own support staff can't open. It covers one section at a time and leaves out audio and video recordings.

I make Cyanote, a notes app that keeps everything on your own disk, so I have an angle. OneNote is free and has a real lock, which many notes apps don't. What follows is a reading of Microsoft's own pages, checked on 23 November 2026.

The short version

  • Encrypted, not end-to-end. Microsoft's OneDrive security page says each file gets its own AES-256 key, and those keys are wrapped by master keys in Microsoft's Azure Key Vault.
  • Software reads your notes, and people can in limited cases. OneDrive content is indexed for search, and automated systems and humans may review content for abuse.
  • Protected sections are the private part. They have limits, and Microsoft itself suggests keeping things like Social Security numbers out of OneNote altogether.
  • I found no full account of the lock's encryption. Two Microsoft help pages say 128-bit AES and stop there.
  • A work account belongs to work. Microsoft's privacy statement says the organisation can access and process your files.

Where your notebooks live

On a personal account a notebook is normally stored in OneDrive. On a work or school account it's in your work OneDrive or SharePoint. Sections are the real files: each section is its own file, and passwords and retention rules both work per section.

There's one way to keep a notebook off Microsoft's servers. OneNote on Windows still supports local notebooks. Microsoft's platform table lists them for Windows only, and not for Mac, iPad, iPhone, Android or the web. A local notebook doesn't sync to your phone, which is the point.

Is OneNote encrypted?

Yes, with Microsoft's keys. For OneDrive, the security page describes TLS between your device and the service and between data centres, plus the per-file keys above. The work version is documented separately: BitLocker on the disks, plus AES-256 on each chunk of each file. Microsoft says its default is to store customers' keys itself, and that keeping your own is an option in many cases, which is mostly a matter for large organisations.

That protects you from someone on your network and from a stolen server disk. It doesn't protect a note from the service, which has to decrypt it to show it in a browser and index it. Google Keep, Notion and Evernote work the same way.

Can Microsoft read your OneNote?

Outside a locked section, technically yes, with rules about when. Three documents set them.

Engineers need approval. The OneDrive page says no engineer has standing access. Access has to be requested, checked and granted for a limited time, and monitoring raises alerts on illicit attempts to reach customer data.

Software processes your content. The Privacy Statement, updated November 2026, says Microsoft collects the content you store in OneDrive to provide, improve and protect the service, and gives indexing your documents for search as an example. It also says your documents and other personal files aren't used to target ads at you.

Abuse checks can involve people. The Services Agreement, in effect since 30 September 2026, covers OneNote.com and OneDrive by name. It says Microsoft may, where applicable, use automated systems and humans to review content for suspected spam, malware, fraud and other illegal or harmful material.

Copilot and training

The Privacy Statement says in general terms that Microsoft may use your data to train AI models. For Microsoft 365 apps there's a narrower promise. Microsoft's page on Copilot in Microsoft 365 apps for home says your prompts, its responses and your file contents aren't used to train foundation models, and that Copilot only uses the file you're in or one you ask it to look at. OneNote is one of the apps it names. That page covers personal accounts; work accounts follow your organisation's contract.

The separate Copilot app depends on its version. Since the one released on 18 August 2026, Microsoft's page for the app makes the same no-training promise for prompts, responses and file contents. Without a subscription you may see ads there, and personalised ones might use your chat history unless you turn that off. The FAQ for the older app says conversations are kept for 18 months by default, some are reviewed by people, and they can train Microsoft's models unless you opt out or are excluded. If you paste a note into the Copilot app, its rules apply to that text.

I found no Microsoft page that says whether Copilot can read a protected section you've unlocked. Assume it can.

What a password-protected section hides

This is OneNote's one strong privacy feature. On Windows you right-click a section and choose Password Protect This Section. On a Mac you Control-click it, then Password Protection > Protect This Section, and lock it in a second step. The web version can unlock a section but not create one.

Before you set a password, store it in a password manager. If you forget it, the notes are gone. Every one of those pages says Microsoft support can't unlock a section.

Which encryption does the lock use?

Microsoft's current answer is 128-bit AES, given on the web and iPhone and iPad pages. The Mac page only says "encryption", and the Windows page doesn't name an algorithm.

Older answers are still online. A 2006 Microsoft blog post says 3DES with a 192-bit key, which describes the OneNote of that time. A developer on Microsoft's Q&A site reported in 2021 AES-256 in files from OneNote Online and AES-128 in OneNote 2010 files, which is one person's finding. A structure in Microsoft's file-format specification holds encryption data that other software is told to ignore.

None of those pages says how your password becomes a key, so you can't judge how well a short one holds up. Use a long one. Microsoft suggests 14 characters or more.

Where the lock stops

  • Recordings. The Windows page says audio and video are separate files that can't have a password. It doesn't say how other attachments are treated.
  • Time. An unlocked section stays open until it times out. On a Mac that's a few minutes and can't be changed. On Windows you can set it.
  • Add-ins. A Windows option lets add-ins read protected sections while they're unlocked.
  • Your device. The Mac and iPhone pages both call passwords "not a foolproof safeguard" against someone holding your device.

Who can reach a OneNote notebook?

Your employer or school. The Privacy Statement says an organisation that gives you a work or school account controls it, and can access and process your communications, files and content. When someone leaves, an admin can create a link to their OneDrive files. Retention policies apply to each OneNote section as its own file, and Microsoft's eDiscovery tools list .one files as supported for text extraction.

A protected section changes less than you'd hope. I found no Microsoft page about eDiscovery and OneNote passwords specifically. For files in general, a password-protected one is listed among the items that can't be fully indexed, and such items can still be collected and exported. So assume the file can be kept, and that you can be asked for the password. Keep personal notes off work accounts.

Courts and police. The Privacy Statement says Microsoft will disclose content, including files in OneDrive, in response to valid legal process. Its government requests report says it requires a warrant or local equivalent for content, and tells users first unless the law forbids it or there are exceptional circumstances, such as an emergency or a hacked account. In the first half of 2026 it received 6,647 demands from US law enforcement for consumer data. That covers all its consumer services, not OneNote alone.

Anyone who signs in as you. One phished password opens every section that isn't locked. First give the account three pieces of security info, as Microsoft recommends. It warns that with two-step on, losing your contact method can mean a 30-day wait or a lost account. Then turn on two-step verification, or remove the password and sign in without one.

Copies that outlive the note

Deleting a page doesn't remove it everywhere at once:

  • Notebook Recycle Bin: 60 days, unless you empty it.
  • OneDrive recycle bin: 30 days on a personal account, 93 on a work or school account unless the admin changes it.
  • Backups on your computer: OneNote makes its own. By default Windows does it daily and keeps two weeks, and the Mac does it weekly and keeps the two latest.

Those local backups are a reason to have BitLocker or FileVault on.

Does Personal Vault help?

Not for a live notebook, as far as Microsoft documents. Personal Vault is a OneDrive folder behind a second identity check, and it locks itself after 20 idle minutes on the web. Its page never mentions OneNote notebooks. They have their own sync, and Microsoft's limits page, written for work accounts, says moving one into a synced folder outside OneNote isn't supported. I wouldn't try it. A PDF of a page is an ordinary file and can go in the vault.

What to change, in order

  1. Secure the account with two-step verification or a passwordless sign-in. It protects every notebook.
  2. Move personal notes off a work account.
  3. Put sensitive pages in one section and lock it. On Windows, go to Account > Options > Advanced > Passwords, tick the box that locks sections as soon as you leave them, and clear the one for add-on programs. On a Mac, ⌥⌘L locks every protected section in the notebook.
  4. Turn Copilot off if you don't want it. On Windows it's File > Options > Copilot > Enable Copilot, in version 2502 or later, on a personal account. Microsoft doesn't list that checkbox for OneNote on a Mac. There, use Preferences > Personal Settings > Privacy and turn off experiences that analyse your content, which disables other features too.
  5. Use a local notebook on Windows for anything that should never be uploaded, and include it in your own backups, since OneDrive won't hold a copy.
  6. Leave some things out. Microsoft's Mac page advises against keeping Social Security numbers and account access codes in OneNote.

Other ways to lock a note are in password-protecting notes on Windows and on a Mac.

Where Cyanote fits

Questions people ask

Can my employer see my personal OneNote on a work laptop?

Not through your work account if the notebook is in a personal Microsoft account. But Microsoft's Privacy Statement says an organisation that manages a Windows device can use its management tools to access data on it, so a personal notebook opened there may be within reach.

Can someone open a protected section without the password?

Microsoft says nobody can unlock it for you. A short password can still be guessed, which is why length matters.
