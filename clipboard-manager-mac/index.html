A clipboard manager for Mac you buy once

Unlimited searchable history, pinned snippets, one global hotkey, and nothing that ever leaves your machine. It is one of seven things Cyanote does, which is either the point or the drawback.

By Lior Rabanian · Prices checked 13 August 2026 · 7 min read
  • Clipboard
  • macOS
  • One-time purchase

macOS has no clipboard history. Copy something, copy something else, and the first thing is gone — not archived, not recoverable, gone. The system holds exactly one item. Every Mac clipboard manager exists to fix that one omission, and they differ mostly in what they do with the record once they start keeping it.

Cyanote's does this: every copy goes into a searchable list held in a local database on your Mac, you open it from any app with one hotkey, and the snippets you paste constantly can be pinned so they never scroll away. It costs $10 once, and the same $10 also buys notes, to-dos, a board, a calendar, habit tracking and routines.

What it does

  • Unlimited history. No cap, no rolling window, no tier that unlocks more of it.
  • Search. Type a few characters of something you copied and it comes back.
  • Pinned snippets. The address, the bank reference, the boilerplate reply — the three things you paste thirty times a week stop scrolling away under ordinary copying.
  • One global hotkey. Works from any app, not only from inside Cyanote.
  • Local only. The history is written to a database on your Mac. There is no account, no upload, and no sync — not as a setting you switch off, but as an absence.

"But Maccy is free"

It is, and it is very good, and if a clipboard manager is genuinely all you want then it is a reasonable place to stop reading.

Maccy is free and open source, local-only, keyboard-first, and it respects the system's concealed-content flag so your password manager's copies are not logged. It is deliberately minimal and text-focused. There is also a $9.99 build on the Mac App Store with the same features, sold as a way to support the developer.

So the honest answer to "why pay?" is not that Maccy is missing something as a clipboard manager. For most people it is not. The answer is that $10 for Cyanote is not really being spent on the clipboard — it is being spent on not needing a separate app for notes, a separate app for tasks, a separate app for habits and a separate app for the calendar, with the clipboard included in that. If you already have all of those solved and just want history, install Maccy. That is a genuine recommendation.

The part worth caring about: what it refuses to keep

Think about what actually passes through your clipboard in an ordinary week. Passwords, on the way from a password manager to a login form. A two-factor code. Your address. A card number. An API key. A private link that grants access to whoever holds it.

A clipboard history turns that stream into a record. That is exactly what makes it useful and exactly what makes it a liability, and how a given tool handles the distinction is the thing worth checking before you install anything — including this one.

Cyanote's position is the blunt one: the history is a local database on your Mac and there is nowhere else for it to go. No account, no server, no sync. That is not a privacy feature bolted on; it is the same decision that makes the app cost $10 once instead of a monthly fee, because there is no infrastructure to pay for. The trade is real and it cuts both ways — the longer discussion of it is here.

What it does not do

  • No sync. Nothing reaches your iPhone, iPad or a second Mac.
  • No image indexing. Paste is built for that; this is not.
  • No shared pinboards. There is no collaboration anywhere in Cyanote.
  • Not on Windows yet. The build exists and is not on sale. When it ships, the same $10 covers it.

If any of those is the feature you came for, one of the other three apps in that table is your answer, and I would rather say so here than take $10 from someone who is about to ask for it back.

The rest of the ten dollars

The clipboard is one surface of seven. The same app holds notes and code notes, to-dos and a board, a calendar, habit tracking, and routines with a Pomodoro timer — all in the same local database, all searchable, no account for any of it.

That matters here for one specific reason: the thing you copied and the thing you are writing are usually related. When the clipboard lives inside the app holding the note, the trip between them is short. It is the least measurable argument on this page and the one I notice most often, so I will state it once and not oversell it.

Requires macOS 12 or later, Apple Silicon or Intel. Fourteen-day refund, no reason needed.
