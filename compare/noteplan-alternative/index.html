A NotePlan alternative you buy once

NotePlan is the closest thing to Cyanote that exists — notes, tasks and a calendar in one place. It is also $99 a year with no way to stop paying.

By Lior Rabanian · Prices checked 14 August 2026 · 8 min read
  • Comparison
  • One-time purchase
  • macOS

Who is writing this. I make Cyanote, so read this as an argument, not a review. Every price is dated and checkable on NotePlan's own pricing page, and the section on what NotePlan does better is the longest one here, because on features it wins more rows than it loses.

The short version: NotePlan combines markdown notes, tasks and a calendar for $12 a month or $99 a year, across Mac, iPad, iPhone and web. Cyanote combines notes, to-dos, a board, a calendar, habits, routines and a system-wide clipboard manager for $10 once, on one Mac, with no account and no sync.

Of every comparison on this site, this is the one where the two products are genuinely aiming at the same thing. So the differences are worth being precise about.

Over five years that is roughly $495 against $10. That gap is large enough that it deserves scrutiny rather than a victory lap, so the next section is about what the $495 is actually buying.

What NotePlan does better than Cyanote

It syncs, and it is on your phone. Mac, iPad, iPhone and web. Capture a thought on a phone at a bus stop and it is on the Mac when you sit down. Cyanote is one app on one Mac, full stop. For a lot of people this single row ends the comparison, and it should.

Your notes are plain markdown files. This is a real architectural advantage and worth understanding. NotePlan's notes are files you can open in any text editor, back up with any tool, and read in thirty years without NotePlan existing. Cyanote keeps notes in a local database — still on your own disk, still exportable, but a database rather than a folder of text. If plain-text durability is your principle, NotePlan honours it more directly.

Calendar-first daily planning. NotePlan is built around the daily note wired to your actual calendar, and it is very good at that specific workflow. Cyanote has a calendar; it is not organised around it the way NotePlan is.

Plugins and a community. NotePlan is extensible and has an active user base writing things for it. Cyanote ships what it ships, and one person decides what that is.

It is a funded product with a team. That is what the subscription pays for, and it is not nothing — it buys continuity, support and a roadmap. Cyanote is one person.

What Cyanote has that NotePlan does not

Three things, and the third is the one nothing else on the market offers.

Habit tracking and routines. Built in, with a Pomodoro timer. In NotePlan, habits are something you construct out of templates and recurring tasks — workable, but you are building it.

A kanban board. Cyanote has one as a first-class view of the same to-dos.

A system-wide clipboard manager. NotePlan does not have one, and neither does any comparable all-in-one app — not Notion, not Agenda, not Lunatask, not Brite. It is the piece that reliably stays a separate subscription, and in Cyanote it is in the same window as the notes.

So the comparison is not quite "$99/year against $10". If NotePlan covers your notes, tasks and calendar, and you are also paying for a habit tracker and a clipboard manager alongside it, the real stack is larger than one line on the bill — which is precisely the arithmetic that produced this app.

When Cyanote is the better buy

You work on one Mac. Sync is most of what the subscription funds. If you never open NotePlan anywhere but your desk, you are paying $99 a year for infrastructure you do not use.

You want habits and a clipboard in the same app. Otherwise they are two more apps beside NotePlan.

You want to stop re-deciding. A subscription is a decision you make once and the bank makes for you every month after. At $99 a year that decision deserves an annual review, and most people never give it one.

When to stay with NotePlan

  • You use the phone app. There is no substitute here — none.
  • Plain-text files are a principle, not a preference. NotePlan's folder of markdown beats a database on that specific ground.
  • Calendar-first planning is your method. NotePlan is built around it; Cyanote merely includes a calendar.
  • You rely on plugins. Nothing here extends.
  • You want a team behind the software. Reasonable, and Cyanote cannot offer it.

Moving your notes over

Easier than most migrations, because NotePlan already stores notes as markdown files on disk.

  1. Find your NotePlan notes folder — they are ordinary .md files.
  2. In Cyanote, open Backup & Restore and choose Import Markdown. It reports what came in, what was skipped as a duplicate, and anything it could not read.

Headings, paragraphs, bold, italic, inline code, fenced code blocks, bullet, numbered and nested lists, task lists, blockquotes, rules and links all survive. Images are not imported — the alt text is kept and you get a warning naming the file. Tables arrive as the literal characters typed. NotePlan's front matter and its > date links are not interpreted; they stay as text. Nothing is silently dropped: anything the importer does not understand is left in the note exactly as written, because a note that reads oddly can be fixed in a minute and a note that arrived half-empty cannot.

What will not come across is the calendar wiring. NotePlan's daily notes are tied to real calendar events; Cyanote's calendar is its own. Expect to rebuild that habit rather than migrate it.

The honest summary

NotePlan is the better product on features, and if you use it on a phone the comparison is over before it starts. What it will not do is stop charging you, and what it does not include is the habit tracking and the clipboard manager you are probably paying someone else for.

Cyanote is the trade where you give up sync, mobile and plugins, and get the whole week in one app on one machine for the price of a sandwich, once. Whether that is a good trade is a question about how you work, not about which app is better.
